# Commercial notes: pricing and the subcontractor pipeline

These notes review the pricing and marketing suggestions against the financial model in
`business/Site_Safety_Financial_Projection_Canada.xlsx`. They are planning notes, not legal advice.

## Per-jobsite pricing

The suggestion is a flat **$350 CAD a month per active jobsite**, with unlimited users. That's
$4,200 a year per site.

- **It fits the model:** the base case already prices by size of contractor. It works out to
  about $2,500 a year per jobsite in 2027, with pilot discounts, and about $5,300 by 2031. $350 a
  month sits inside that range, so it doesn't break the projection.
- **Unlimited users is right for this product:** it needs reports from everyone on site, so a
  price per user would discourage the people whose input the model needs.
- **Keep the onboarding fee:** linking schedules and logs and training supervisors is real work.
  The model assumes $1,500 to $25,000 depending on size.
- **Treat it as a test price:** no buyer has seen any price yet. Quote it to the pilot contractors
  and see whether $350 is too low for large sites, where the value per site is higher.

## The subcontractor tier and email campaign

A $50 a month tier for subcontractors who act as prime contractor on their own jobs is a sensible
product. The suggested way to sell it, though, carries legal risk.

- **Don't collect emails from the anonymous worker portal.** It has to stay anonymous, or workers
  stop reporting hazards.
- **Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL)** covers commercial emails, and penalties can reach
  $10 million per violation for a business. Emailing a subcontractor's staff a sales pitch because
  their address was captured while they logged work for a general contractor is unlikely to count
  as consent. Their business relationship is with the general contractor, not with you.
- **Do it with consent instead:** when a subcontractor company creates its own login, add an
  unticked box: "Send me occasional product news about Site Safety Early Warning." Record when and
  how consent was given. Every email must name the sender and have a working unsubscribe link.
- **Avoid statistics nobody has checked:** a claim such as "5,000 safe hours under our engine"
  implies the product kept people safe. The product is not validated on real outcomes yet, so
  don't state or imply that it prevented injuries.

Have a Canadian lawyer review the consent wording and the marketing plan before launch.
