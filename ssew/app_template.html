Site Safety Early Warning

Which zone of a construction site is most likely to hurt someone in the next five work days, the warning signs behind it, and the controls to put in place before the shift. Calibrated to WorkSafeBC, AWCBC, Statistics Canada and Alberta OHS data, run on real Environment Canada weather, and re-scored live as a site logs each day.

Independent analysis by a private analyst. Not produced by, affiliated with or endorsed by WorkSafeBC, the WSIB, Ontario's Ministry of Labour, Alberta OHS or any employer.

Before you upload

  • Where the photos go: to Claude, on your own Claude account, for this analysis only. The page doesn't store them, and the downloaded report keeps them only if you save it.
  • People in photos: Claude is told to judge equipment and conditions, never to identify anyone. Avoid close-up faces and name badges where you can.
  • It's a second pair of eyes. Locations are approximate and a photo shows one moment. A competent person confirms each finding before acting; that confirmation is what goes into the log.
  • Best results: wide shots of the work area in good light, one zone per batch.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

Three provinces, one method

Each province's sites run on its own real weather and are calibrated to its own construction lost-time claims rate (AWCBC claims ÷ Statistics Canada construction employment). Boards differ in coverage and in what counts as lost time, so rates compare each province with itself, not with the others. Controls cite each province's own legislation.

Alert cut-offs set for each province

One shared cut-off under-flagged low-rate Ontario and Alberta: their riskiest zones rarely reached BC-level risk. Each province now alerts at the lower of the shared cut-off and its own top 5% (red) and next 15% (amber) of training zone-days, so no province is flagged less than before.
